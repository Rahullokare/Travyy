# Travyy: market research, positioning and monetisation

_Second edition, 16 November 2026. Replaces the October 2026 edition (in git history). Figures marked "estimate" come from third-party trackers or vendor blogs and disagree with each other; re-check them before they go into a pitch deck._

## 1. What changed since October

The October edition said the reels-to-map category was "validated but not won" and that Travyy should compete on the visit rate. Three things have moved since:

1. **Reels-to-map is now a feature, not a product.** A November search turns up 20+ apps doing the same thing (Triply, Plotline, ReelTravel, Dreamfare, Dream Trip, Wandereel, Strollio, Map Your Voyage, Tote, Travio, Via, Wanderbox, Pintra, Rhyme…), and the big players have added it: Expedia reads travel Reels and suggests stays, and Mindtrip ($22.5M raised, backed by Capital One Ventures and United Airlines Ventures) bought the creator-guide platform Thatch and now books flights in-chat. Most "best app" rankings are written by the vendors themselves.
2. **Nobody in the long tail has broken out.** The listings still show a handful of ratings each. The funded players are moving toward B2B (Mindtrip sells to destination marketing organisations), which is what consumer travel apps do when consumers won't pay enough.
3. **Travyy now has something the others don't:** offline, multi-currency cost splitting inside the trip (shipped 6 November). No reels-to-map app has it, and no splitting app has a map or a plan.

So the October positioning ("the places you actually go to") describes a real outcome but no longer separates us from 20 apps that say nearly the same thing.

### What the numbers say

- **Demand is real.** 48% of Gen Z travellers in Spain, Italy and the UK name TikTok as a source of trip ideas (Euronews, August 2026); Skyscanner Australia reports 59% for TikTok and 51% for Instagram.
- **Group trips are the fastest-growing kind in our launch market.** Airbnb reports Indian Gen Z group bookings up almost 55% year on year, seven in ten preferring several short trips to one long one, and 66% booking within days or weeks of travel. Skyscanner: 87% of Indian Gen Z find booking a trip overwhelming, against 52% globally.
- **Travel subscriptions churn and come back.** RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115,000 apps) calls out travel for its churn-and-return pattern: people pay around a trip and leave. Freemium apps convert a median 2.1% of trials to paid by day 35, hard paywalls 10.7%; longer trials convert better (42.5% for 17–32 days against 25.5% for four days or fewer); 55% of trial cancellations happen on day one.
- **Bookings pay without a subscription.** Tours and activities programmes (GetYourGuide, Viator) pay roughly 8% of the booking; Klook up to about 8%; Booking.com shares part of its own commission, which works out to about 4–5% of the stay.

## 3. Positioning

> **Travyy is the group trip app: from the reel in the group chat to the last rupee settled.**
> Everyone shares the reels they've saved, the spots land on one map, Travyy turns them into a day-by-day plan, and the costs are split as you go.

Why this, and not "best reels-to-map app":

1. **It is the only claim we can own today.** Capture + plan + split in one app exists nowhere else. Extraction quality is a race against funded teams that we'd lose.
2. **Groups distribute the product.** A solo save tool grows one install at a time. A trip brings three to six people, and the plan and the money summary are both things one person sends to the rest.
3. **It attacks two incumbents at their weakest point.** Against reels apps: "then what?" Against Splitwise: no daily cap, no account needed, and the costs sit on the stops they paid for.
4. **It fits the launch market.** Android-first, short group trips booked late, a population that says planning is overwhelming. Almost all of the long tail is iPhone-only.

The visit rate stays as the personal payoff (the profile screen still leads with it), but it's no longer the headline.

**Who it's for first:** 20–32-year-olds in India planning 2–5-day trips with friends (Goa, Jaipur, Bali, Thailand, Vietnam), organised by one person who does all the work. That organiser is our user; the rest of the group meets Travyy through the link they're sent.

## 4. Monetisation

Three streams, in the order they start paying.

### 4.1 Free must stay generous where it spreads the app

| | Free | Trip Pass | Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saved spots | Unlimited | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Split costs, any currency | Unlimited | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Share a trip as a link | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| AI imports (reads caption, cover and video) | 15 a month, then on-device | Unlimited for 30 days | Unlimited |
| Planned trips | 1 | +1 per pass | Unlimited |
| Price | — | $5.99 · ₹249 once | $29.99/yr (7-day trial) · ₹999/yr; $4.99/mo · ₹199/mo |

Changes from October, and why:

- **The 30-save cap is gone.** Saving is the habit and the top of the funnel; capping it punished exactly the behaviour we need. What we meter instead is what costs us money (AI calls) and what's worth money (planning a second trip).
- **Running out of AI imports never blocks a save.** The app reads the caption on the phone instead and says so. A blocked share from Instagram is a lost user; a slightly worse result with an explanation is an upsell.
- **Splitting has no cap, ever.** It's the wedge against Splitwise and the reason a whole group installs.
- **Trip Pass is the main paid product, not a footnote.** People who travel twice a year won't subscribe, and RevenueCat's data says travel subscribers leave after the trip anyway. A one-off price near a coffee, bought at the moment someone starts planning trip two, matches how they think.
- **India gets India prices.** ₹999/yr is under half the dollar price; the stores support per-country pricing and the paywall shows what the store returns.

Working assumption for the model: over a year, 3% of monthly actives buy something, two thirds of them a pass. At 10,000 monthly actives that's about 200 passes ($1,200) and 100 yearly subscriptions ($3,000) a year before store fees, roughly $350 a month. Subscriptions alone won't carry the company at that scale, which is why 4.2 matters.

### 4.2 Booking commission: revenue from people who never pay

Every saved sight, activity and stay is a booking someone intends to make. Travyy now shows:

- on a saved **sight, activity or nature spot**: tickets and tours for that place (GetYourGuide);
- on a saved **stay**: prices for it (Booking.com);
- on a **trip**: stays and things to do in that city.

Rules: only on places the user saved or in a trip they planned, labelled as partner links, never on food or cafés where there's nothing to book. At an average activity booking of $60 and 8%, each booking is about $5, the same as a Trip Pass, and it needs no purchase decision. One booking per 25 monthly actives would already match subscription revenue.

### 4.3 Later

- **Creator guides.** A travel creator publishes their reel's spots as a Travyy trip link; followers open it with one tap. Mindtrip buying Thatch says this channel has value. For us it's distribution first, a revenue share on bookings second.
- **Group Pro.** One person pays and the whole trip gets Pro. Needs live group sync (see roadmap).

### Unit costs

Gemini and Groq free tiers cover extraction today. On paid Gemini Flash-Lite a caption-plus-cover import costs a fraction of a cent and a video import a few cents at most, so 15 free imports a month is affordable and unlimited imports at ₹999/yr still leaves margin. Per-account rate limits on the server are already in place.

## 5. Go-to-market

1. **The share link is the growth loop.** Every trip can be sent as a link that opens on a map in the browser, with "Add to my Travyy". No install, no account. Every organiser exposes the product to their whole group.
2. **The money summary is the second loop.** "You owe Priya ₹1,240 — settled with Travyy" goes into the group chat after every trip.
3. **Demo on the platforms where the problem lives.** Reels showing "I shared this Goa reel and got a 3-day plan and a split in 20 seconds".
4. **App-store search.** "Splitwise alternative no limit", "group trip planner", "save reels to map" — the first is the least contested and the most motivated.
5. **Creators,** once share links have data: a link that opens their guide beats "location in comments".

## 6. What to measure

| Metric | Target | Event |
|---|---|---|
| First real save in first session | > 50% | `places_saved` |
| Trip planned within 14 days | > 25% | `trip_created` |
| Trips shared as a link | > 30% of trips | `trip_shared` |
| Shared link → import | > 15% of opens | `shared_trip_opened`, `shared_trip_imported` |
| Trips with a ledger | > 40% | `ledger_created` |
| Booking link taps per planned trip | > 0.5 | `booking_opened` |
| Paywall view → purchase | > 8% | `paywall_viewed`, `purchase_completed` |
| People per trip (ledger members) | > 3 | `ledger_created.people` |

The north star moves from visit rate to **trips completed with three or more people**, because it captures capture, planning, the group and the money in one number.

## 7. Roadmap

Built in this round: the new plans and AI quota, the billing seam, booking links, share links, and analytics delivery to PostHog.

Next, in order:

1. **Store billing (RevenueCat).** Needs Play Console and App Store products; the app-side seam is `features/paywall/billing.ts`.
2. **Live group trips.** Supabase tables with row-level security; members join from the share link; everyone adds spots and expenses. This turns the link from a copy into a shared space and unlocks Group Pro.
3. **Voting on spots,** so the group decides and the organiser stops refereeing.
4. **Nearby alerts** in the background (geofencing and notifications).
5. **UPI deep links** on "settle up" for India.
6. **Affiliate accounts:** apply to GetYourGuide and Booking.com partner programmes and set the two partner IDs. The links work without them, unpaid.
